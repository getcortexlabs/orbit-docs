On this page

Wiki ​

Open Wiki in the sidebar to read and maintain your team's runbooks, project decisions and incident notes. With no page open, the wiki shows the collection as a small sky: each folder of the top level is a planet around the number of pages, and Ted, Orbit's astronaut, floats beside them with a line that changes with the day. Below come the pages edited last, the folders with how many pages they hold, and the pages at the top level.

Gathering cosmic dust lists up to three pages with no edit in over two months, the oldest first, so someone checks whether they still hold.

In the wiki, Orbit's own sidebar steps aside to leave room for reading. Menu, at the top left, shows it again, and the choice is remembered in that browser. The library on the left folds away too, with the button in its header or with Command+\ on macOS and Ctrl+\ on other systems.

Use New folder to organize a scope. Open a folder to create pages or nested folders inside it. To move an existing item, edit it and change its parent. The Markdown ZIP includes empty folders and preserves the hierarchy.

Choose a collection ​

General is shared by signed-in users across the installation. Anyone who is admin or owner of a workspace edits it, and so does Orbit's administrator. Workspace holds knowledge shared by members of one workspace. Project keeps a collection for a single project. Its overview links straight to its pages. Workspace owners and admins can edit both collections. Viewers and developers can read pages, follow resource links, view history and export.

Write and organize pages ​

Choose New page, enter a title, and select a parent to nest it in the tree. Leave the parent at Top level for a root page. Write in Markdown. While you write, the wiki takes the whole width and says how many words the page has and how long it takes to read. On a wide screen the page shows beside the text as you type: Side by side turns that off, and Preview shows the page alone, to check headings, lists, links, tables and code blocks. Choose Save page or press Command+Enter on macOS or Ctrl+Enter on other systems. On small screens, open Library to browse the tree.

To start faster, choose a template: Runbook, Decision record, Incident report, Onboarding notes or Workspace architecture. The page opens with its sections in place, and you can change anything before saving. A list line that starts with [ ] or [x], such as - [ ] Check the latest backup, shows as a checklist item.

Use Link a resource in the editor to insert an application, database, server, Space or domain. The editor stores its stable identifier, such as [[database:svc_example]]. The reader shows its current name, status and link. A deleted or inaccessible resource appears as unavailable. Incident and warning views link to pages mentioning the affected resource.

Choose Image to insert an HTTPS image URL or upload a file. Add a description for readers using assistive technology. PNG, JPEG and WebP uploads can be up to 10 MiB and larger images are resized to fit the page. Animated GIFs can be up to 180 KiB. Uploaded images travel with the Markdown export and revision history.

Open a folder and choose Move to another collection to move its whole tree and history to another collection. The folder appears at the destination's top level. Moves involving General take an admin of the workspace on the other side of the move. A move into General makes the entire tree and its history readable by every signed-in user.

Paste a copied image into the Markdown editor with Ctrl+V or Command+V to insert it at the cursor.

Read a page ​

A page opens with a cover drawn from the page itself, so each one has its own. As you scroll, Ted flies along the top to show how far you are, and On this page follows the section you are in. Code blocks have a Copy button, and an address written as plain text opens as a link in a new tab. At the end, the wiki offers the previous and the next page of the same folder.

Restrict a folder ​

When you create or rename a folder, Who can see this folder decides who it is for. Owners and admins only hides the folder and everything inside it from the other members: it leaves their library, their search and their export.

Ask for a password to open adds a password of at least 8 characters. Someone who could already see the folder types it once and the folder stays open for 30 minutes in that session. Lock now closes it sooner. A password never gives access to someone who could not see the folder, and after five wrong tries in a row Orbit asks that person to wait before trying again.

If the password is forgotten, choose Forgot the password? on the locked folder. An owner of the workspace, or Orbit's administrator, removes the password there without knowing it, and the removal is recorded in the activity. In General, an owner of any workspace can do it.

A password is a lock on the door, not encryption: the text is stored like any other page and is part of Orbit's backups. API tokens and AI agents never open a password folder and never see inside one. They see a folder for owners and admins only when their role is admin.

A folder keeps its restriction when it moves to another collection. A folder that sits inside a password folder has to be moved out of it first.

Draw the workspace's architecture ​

In a workspace or project collection, the Workspace architecture template asks Orbit to describe how things run. It reads the projects, applications, databases and Spaces, their domains, the servers they run on, and the variables that reference another service, then opens a page with:

  • a diagram: the internet, each domain pointing at its application, each environment as a group, and an arrow from an application to every database or service it references;
  • a section per environment listing each service with its source, domain, replicas, servers and what it uses, where the names show their status live;
  • the servers with the ones that receive traffic;
  • how the page was made.

Previews of pull requests appear in the lists but not in the diagram. Review the page and save it like any other. To update it later, use the template again: the previous version stays in the page's history.

Orbit draws only what it manages. A connection typed by hand in an ordinary variable, or written in the code, does not appear, so add it to the page yourself if it matters.

Any page can draw a diagram: write it in a code block whose language is mermaid.

Find a runbook ​

Choose Search, or press Command+K on macOS, Ctrl+K on other systems, or /, to find a page in the current collection by title or text. Title matches appear as you type, with the folder each page lives in, and matches in the text follow. The arrow keys move through the results and Enter opens one. When nothing matches, the same list offers to create a page or a folder with the words you typed. The console command palette also searches the general wiki and your active workspace's pages, including its projects.

Review or restore a revision ​

Each save records the author and time. Open History, select a revision, then choose Restore as a new revision to bring it back while retaining later edits in history. If someone saves while you're writing, Orbit shows the saved version beside your draft. Review both, continue editing your draft and save when ready.

Delete and restore a page ​

Delete on a page moves it to the trash after you confirm. The page leaves the library, the search and the export. Its text and every revision are kept, so nothing is lost by deleting. A folder has to be empty first: move or delete its pages, then delete the folder.

Trash, under the library, lists what was deleted in the collection you are in, with who deleted each page and when. Restore brings a page back to its folder, or to the top of the collection when that folder is gone. A page in the trash cannot be edited until it is restored.

Deleting and restoring take the same role as writing: admin in the workspace, and admin of any workspace in General. The same holds for API tokens and AI agents: one with the admin role writes General, one with a lower role only reads it. Orbit does not empty the trash by itself.

Download and recover ​

Export Markdown downloads a ZIP with the latest version of every page in the selected scope. Workspace exports include project folders. Page folders follow the tree and filenames include the page ID. The ZIP contains Markdown source and stable resource references. Orbit's own database holds the full pages and their revision history, so its control-plane backups include both.
