On this page

Notifications ​

Orbit tells a Slack or Discord channel, or a list of email addresses, when something needs you: a failed deployment or backup, an incident, a failover, a server going offline, or a warning before something breaks, such as a disk filling up. You do not have to keep the console open to find out.

Adding a Slack channel ​

  1. In Slack, create an app for your workspace, turn on Incoming Webhooks and add a webhook to the channel that should receive the messages. Slack gives you a URL that starts with https://hooks.slack.com/services/.
  2. In Orbit, open Settings → Notifications → Add Slack channel, give the channel a name, paste the URL and choose the events it receives.
  3. Click Send test. Orbit sends one message right away and shows what Slack answered, so a wrong or revoked webhook shows up now and not during an incident.

Adding a Discord channel ​

  1. In Discord, edit the channel that should receive the messages, open Integrations → Webhooks and create a webhook. This needs the Manage Webhooks permission. Copy Webhook URL gives a URL that starts with https://discord.com/api/webhooks/.
  2. In Orbit, open Settings → Notifications → Add Discord channel, give the channel a name, paste the URL and choose the events it receives.
  3. Click Send test.

Messages arrive as embeds colored by severity (red for failures, amber for warnings, green when something is resolved), and never mention anyone, so @everyone in a reason cannot ping the channel.

Setting up email ​

Email goes through an SMTP server you already have: your provider's, or a service such as Amazon SES, Postmark, Mailgun or a mailbox's own SMTP. Orbit uses one server for every workspace, and only Orbit's administrator sets it up.

  1. Open Settings → Email → Set up email.
  2. Enter the server and port, the connection (STARTTLS, usually port 587, or TLS, usually 465), the user and password, and the address to send from.
  3. Click Send a test and save. Orbit sends a message to you, or to the address you type, with those settings and saves them only if the server accepts it. Otherwise it shows what the server answered, such as 535 Authentication credentials invalid.
  • The password is sealed in Orbit's secret store, never returned and never written to the logs. Leave the field empty when changing the other settings to keep it.
  • Unencrypted connections are only for a server on a private network, and cannot send a user and password.
  • Invitations to a workspace are emailed too once email is set up. See Members and roles.

Sending through Resend ​

Instead of an SMTP server, email can go through Resend's API. Orbit then sets up the sending domain, publishes its records where it manages the zone, and stops emailing addresses that bounce or complain. One provider sends at a time; the SMTP settings stay saved when you switch.

  1. In Resend, create an API key with full access under API Keys. A key that may only send cannot set up domains, and Orbit refuses it. Paste it under Settings → Email → Resend → Connect Resend. Orbit checks it by listing the account's domains, seals it in the secret store and never shows it again.
  2. Choose a sending domain, a domain or a subdomain such as mail.example.com, and the region Resend sends from, São Paulo included. Orbit creates it at Resend, or uses it when the account already has it, and shows the records Resend asks for: a DKIM key, the SPF pair (MX and TXT on the send subdomain) and a DMARC policy Orbit recommends.
  3. When the domain's zone is on Cloudflare, DigitalOcean or Hostinger through Orbit, Orbit publishes the records itself, asks Resend to verify them, and follows the status every two minutes until Resend settles it. They show on the DNS page as Orbit's. A name that already has a record of the same purpose, such as a DMARC policy of your own on the domain or on its zone, is kept as it is. Otherwise, copy the records where the domain's DNS is and click Check again.
  4. Once the domain is verified, choose the address to send from, on that domain, and where replies go. Send a test and use Resend sends a test email first and switches email to Resend only if Resend accepts it.

Alerts and invitations then go through Resend. Each alert carries tags with its kind and its delivery, and a retry of the same delivery is sent once. Answers that a retry cannot fix, such as an invalid recipient, an unverified domain, a revoked key or a plan limit, stop the retries at once with Resend's own words; the others are retried as usual.

What happened after sending: when Orbit has a public HTTPS address, it registers a Resend webhook for sent, delivered, delayed, bounced and complained email and checks the signature of every event. The latest deliveries in Settings → Notifications show whether Resend delivered each email. An address that bounces for good, or whose owner marks Orbit's email as spam, is marked: the email channel and the invitation say so, Orbit stops sending to it, and Settings → Email lists it with Email again to clear the mark.

Send a test in Settings → Email checks the provider in use at any time. Use SMTP again switches back to the saved SMTP server, and Disconnect forgets the key and the webhook; the domain and its records stay at Resend and in DNS.

Adding an email channel ​

  1. Once email is set up, open Settings → Notifications → Add email channel.
  2. Give it a name, type up to 20 addresses separated by commas or new lines, and choose the events it receives.
  3. Click Send test.

Messages have a plain text part and a simple HTML part. The subject starts with [Orbit] and names what happened and where, and both parts link to the right page in the console when Orbit has a public HTTPS address.

Channels ​

  • Where the URL lives: it is sealed in Orbit's secret store, never shown again and never written to the logs or to delivery errors. It travels with orbit backup. To change it, edit the channel and paste a new one.
  • Several channels: add as many as you like, for example one per team, each with its own events.
  • Workspaces: each Orbit workspace has its own channels, and only hears about its own projects. Servers and Orbit updates are shared, so they reach the channels of every workspace that follows them.

Warnings before something breaks ​

Orbit checks every minute for things that are about to break, and lists the open ones under Warnings on the Overview. A channel that follows a warning gets one message when it opens and another when it clears, so a disk that stays full does not repeat itself.

  • A database's storage size is requested, not enforced: a database that passes it keeps growing into the server's disk, so its warning comes before the disk's.
  • Measurements come from Kubernetes on each server. A server that is offline has no new measurements, so its warnings stay as they were; going offline is its own event.
  • New channels follow every warning. A channel created before warnings existed follows none of them until you tick them in its settings.
  • Resolved warnings stay listed for 7 days.

Repeats and retries ​

  • One message per event. Orbit remembers what it already sent, so a restart does not send it again. After being stopped, it picks up where it left off. If it was stopped for long, it skips what it missed instead of flooding the channel.
  • Repeats are grouped. When the same thing happens again within ten minutes (the same service failing to deploy, the same database failing its backup), the first message goes out at once and the rest arrive as one follow-up at the end of those ten minutes, with how many more times it happened.
  • Failed deliveries are retried after 30 seconds, 2, 10 and 30 minutes and 2 hours. When Slack or Discord says the webhook no longer exists or is not valid, or the email server refuses an address for good (a 5xx answer), Orbit stops at once.
  • Rate limits delay messages instead of losing them. Orbit sends a channel's messages one after the other and follows the limits Discord announces, so a burst waits its turn. When Slack or Discord still answers that it is rate limited, Orbit waits as long as it asks without counting it as a failed attempt, for up to a day.
  • Recent messages in Settings lists the latest deliveries with their status and what Slack or Discord answered. Each channel shows when its last message was sent or why it failed. Deliveries are kept for 30 days.

Weekly summary ​

Open Settings → Notifications → Your week in Orbit to preview the last seven days. Previewing does not send mail. An administrator can enable the installation schedule after configuring SMTP or Resend, then choose a weekday and time in the installation time zone. A user's display time zone does not move delivery. Scheduling starts disabled when Orbit is upgraded.

Administrators receive the summary by default once scheduling is enabled. Members can opt in. Each person can change Email me a weekly summary in their profile or Notifications; mail goes only to their account address. Reports contain only accessible workspaces. Fleet prices, server diagnostics, active-server changes and blocked-address counts are administrator-only. Pending deliveries are cancelled when the recipient, membership, subscription or installation setting changes. The last attempt's status appears in Notifications.

The preview and scheduled email use the same report builder:

  • Costs show current recorded monthly server prices and a proportional month-to-date estimate, not an invoice. Servers without a price are identified. Last month's billing comparison is unavailable because Orbit has no monthly billing ledger.
  • Resource recommendations require the existing complete seven-day measurements and safety checks. Retained CPU and memory reductions use confirmed optimization baselines, including reversals; they are capacity, not invoice savings.
  • Backups distinguish a retained copy tested this week, an older tested copy, and no retained copy with a successful restore test.
  • Availability shows successful checks across the previous seven complete UTC days and incident durations within the report window. Missing checks are not counted as uptime.
  • Applications show deploy attempts and failures, observed restarts and memory-exhaustion restarts. Missing metrics are reported as unknown.
  • Servers show their latest reported state, the disk sample's one-hour window and k3s's 24-hour window, with observation times. Replication is current state. Active-server changes are recorded from this version onward; an incomplete first week is identified, and a restart on the same server does not count as failover.
  • Warnings include still-open warnings and those opened in the period. Email counts cover notifications in current email channels, with the provider's known outcomes and newly suppressed addresses; SMTP cannot confirm delivery. Large sections are shortened with a link to the console.

A quiet installation sends a short message instead of empty sections. The active Orbit persists one pending report per person, retries temporary failures, and catches up only within 24 hours of the scheduled time. The same local calendar week is not queued again after a restart or a repeated daylight-saving hour. Nonexistent daylight-saving hours follow the backup scheduler's next-hour rule. Reports and provider idempotency keys stay fixed during retries. Resend deduplicates retries using that key; SMTP may duplicate a message if the process stops after SMTP accepts it but before Orbit records success. Bounced or complained-about recipients remain blocked by the existing email suppression list.

Emails currently use English, like the existing notification emails. The console preview is translated into English and Brazilian Portuguese.
